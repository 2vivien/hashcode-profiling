# Assessment V1 questionnaire — revision V2

The 40-item questionnaire was revised after comparing its dimensions with the O*NET 31.0 content model and ESCO's occupation/skill matching model.

## Design goals

- closed responses only;
- broad coverage of all ISCO-08 occupational families rather than a technology-first list;
- explicit signals for interests, abilities, learning strategy, values, work context, adaptability and constraints;
- several task/environment questions that can distinguish technical, scientific, creative, social, business, administrative, agricultural, industrial and operational paths;
- no question directly assigns a job title;
- downstream matching uses occupation evidence from ESCO/O*NET instead of questionnaire labels.

## Why the revision matters

O*NET's content model separates career interests, abilities, work styles, skills, knowledge, education/training, work activities and work context. The questionnaire therefore does not rely on RIASEC alone.

ESCO is designed to connect occupations to skills and competences and can also connect labour-market profiles to training. The questionnaire therefore captures both person-level preferences and constraints needed to explain skill gaps and training paths.

## Forty-question structure

- Q01–Q06: six career-interest types;
- Q07–Q12: activity, environment, outcome, problem and learning preferences;
- Q13–Q18: numerical, verbal, logical, technical-learning, problem-solving and communication self-efficacy;
- Q19–Q24: subjects, trajectory, persistence and learning strategies;
- Q25–Q32: values, human interaction, physical activity, structure, teamwork, rhythm, mobility and uncertainty;
- Q33–Q36: adaptability, agency, decisions under uncertainty and metacognition;
- Q37–Q40: training horizon, constraints, financial risk and current career clarity.

The instrument is still an engineering assessment and is not a clinically or psychometrically validated test. Real Otheloo pilot data is required to estimate reliability, calibrate weights and evaluate predictive validity.
